    Quality check the raw reads

    FASTQ files store each read as four lines: a name, the bases, a + separator, and one quality character per base. fastqc summarizes quality across all reads.

    fastqc -t $THREADS -o qc $R1 $R2
    Look inside

    Print the first read:

    zcat $R1 | head -4

    Each quality character encodes a Phred score. printf '%d\n' "'F" prints 70, and 70 − 33 = Q37: roughly a 1-in-5,000 chance that the base is wrong.

    ngs_ws_R1_fastqc.html
    2

    Trim and filter the reads

    fastp removes adapter sequence and low-quality read ends, and drops reads that become too short.

    fastp -i $R1 -I $R2 -o $SAMPLE.trim_R1.fq.gz -O $SAMPLE.trim_R2.fq.gz -w $THREADS -h qc/$SAMPLE.fastp.html -j qc/$SAMPLE.fastp.json
    Look inside

    Compare reads and Q30 before and after filtering:

    jq .summary qc/$SAMPLE.fastp.json
    ngs_ws.fastp.html
    3

    Align the reads to the genome

    bwa finds where each read pair comes from in the 3-billion-base GRCh38 genome. -R tags every read with the sample name. This takes a couple of minutes, mostly to load the genome index into memory.

    bwa mem -v 1 -t $THREADS -R "@RG\tID:$SAMPLE\tSM:$SAMPLE\tPL:ILLUMINA" $REF $SAMPLE.trim_R1.fq.gz $SAMPLE.trim_R2.fq.gz > $SAMPLE.sam
    Look inside

    SAM is plain text with one line per read. Columns 1–9 are the read name, flag, chromosome, position, mapping quality (MAPQ), CIGAR (how the read lines up, e.g. 140M = 140 matching bases) and mate information:

    grep -v '^@' $SAMPLE.sam | head -3 | cut -f1-9
    4

    Convert to BAM, sort and index

    BAM is the compressed binary form of SAM. Sorting by position and indexing let tools jump straight to any region.

    samtools view -b -o $SAMPLE.raw.bam $SAMPLE.sam
    samtools sort -@ $THREADS -o $SAMPLE.sorted.bam $SAMPLE.raw.bam
    samtools index $SAMPLE.sorted.bam
    Look inside

    Compare file sizes, then count the reads covering KRAS codon 12:

    ls -lh $SAMPLE.sam $SAMPLE.*.bam
    samtools view -c $SAMPLE.sorted.bam chr12:25245350
    5

    Mark PCR duplicates

    Library PCR makes identical copies of some fragments. Copies are not independent evidence, so sambamba flags them (SAM flag 1024) and the variant callers ignore them.

    sambamba markdup -t $THREADS $SAMPLE.sorted.bam $SAMPLE.dedup.bam 2> qc/$SAMPLE.markdup.log
    Look inside

    How many reads are now marked as duplicates?

    samtools flagstat $SAMPLE.dedup.bam
    6

    Realign reads around indels

    Aligners place reads one at a time, so reads carrying an insertion or deletion are often misaligned near its ends. ABRA2 assembles the reads in each target region and realigns them together.

    mkdir -p ~/tmp
    abra2 --in $SAMPLE.dedup.bam --out $SAMPLE.realigned.bam --ref $REF --targets $BED --threads $THREADS --tmpdir ~/tmp > qc/$SAMPLE.abra2.log 2>&1
    samtools index $SAMPLE.realigned.bam

    From here on, $BAM refers to ngs_ws.realigned.bam.

    Look inside (in IGV, step 12)

    Compare ngs_ws.dedup.bam and ngs_ws.realigned.bam at the FBXW7 frameshift. After ABRA2, more reads show the 1-base deletion instead of mismatches near their ends.

    7

    Check coverage and combine the QC

    Low-VAF variants need deep coverage. mosdepth measures depth across each target region.

    mosdepth -t $THREADS -n -b $BED --thresholds 100,250,500 qc/$SAMPLE $BAM
    samtools flagstat $BAM > qc/$SAMPLE.flagstat.txt

    MultiQC collects every QC result so far into one report:

    multiqc -f -o qc/multiqc qc
    Look inside

    The five targets with the lowest mean depth:

    zcat qc/$SAMPLE.regions.bed.gz | sort -k5,5n | head -5
    multiqc_report.html
    Part 2 · Calling variants in three rounds

    Each round calls variants from the same BAM, annotates them with fastVEP, and scores them against the truth set: the 18 variants known to be in this sample.

    8

    Round 1 · VarScan with a 5% VAF cutoff

    samtools mpileup counts the bases at every position in the targets. VarScan then reports positions where the alternate allele passes its thresholds: at least 5% of reads (--min-var-freq 0.05), at least 8× coverage, and support on both strands.

    samtools mpileup -B -q 20 -Q 30 -d 4000 -l $BED -f $REF $BAM | varscan mpileup2vcf --min-coverage 8 --min-var-freq 0.05 --p-value 0.05 --min-avg-qual 30 --strand-filter 1 --output-vcf 1 --variants > r1.vcf

    Compress and index the VCF so IGV and bcftools can read it:

    bgzip -f r1.vcf && tabix -f -p vcf r1.vcf.gz
    Look inside

    Each call with its VarScan allele frequency (FREQ):

    bcftools query -f '%CHROM:%POS\t%REF>%ALT\t[%FREQ]\n' r1.vcf.gz

    The read counts behind one call, BRAF V600E:

    samtools mpileup -r chr7:140753336-140753336 -f $REF $BAM

    Annotate

    fastVEP adds genes, transcripts, consequences, HGVS names and ClinVar. annotate-vcf prints the full fastvep command before running it.

    annotate-vcf r1.vcf.gz r1
    Look inside

    Gene, consequence and protein change for each call:

    awk -F'\t' -v OFS='\t' '/^##/ {next} !h {for (i = 1; i <= NF; i++) c[$i] = i; h = 1; next} $c["HGVSp"] != "-" {print $c["Location"], $c["SYMBOL"], $c["Consequence"], $c["HGVSp"]}' r1.fastvep.tsv | sort -u | column -t

    r1.fastvep.vcf holds the same information in the VCF's CSQ field.

    Benchmark

    benchmark-vcf r1.vcf.gz
    What to expectMost variants are found, but not EGFR L858R (3%), KRAS G12D (4%) or the FLT3 ITD. BRAF V600E at 6% sits right at the cutoff.
    DiscussIs a 3% EGFR L858R clinically important? What limits how low the cutoff can go?
    9

    Round 2 · VarScan with a 2% VAF cutoff

    The same command with --min-var-freq 0.02, then compress, annotate and benchmark:

    samtools mpileup -B -q 20 -Q 30 -d 4000 -l $BED -f $REF $BAM | varscan mpileup2vcf --min-coverage 8 --min-var-freq 0.02 --p-value 0.05 --min-avg-qual 30 --strand-filter 1 --output-vcf 1 --variants > r2.vcf
    bgzip -f r2.vcf && tabix -f -p vcf r2.vcf.gz
    annotate-vcf r2.vcf.gz r2
    benchmark-vcf r2.vcf.gz
    Look inside

    How many calls did each round make?

    for f in r1.vcf.gz r2.vcf.gz; do echo "$f: $(bcftools view -H $f | wc -l) calls"; done
    What to expectL858R and G12D are now found. Check the "not in the truth set" list: a lower cutoff also lets through sequencing and PCR errors.
    DiscussHow would a clinical lab decide whether a 2% call is real? Why does the FLT3 ITD still not appear?
    10

    Round 3 · VarDict for the FLT3 ITD

    The FLT3 ITD inserts 279 bases, about twice the read length. No read contains the whole insertion, so bwa cannot write it as an insertion. It soft-clips the part of each read that doesn't match the genome instead. mpileup only counts aligned bases, so VarScan never sees the ITD at any cutoff.

    Look inside

    Reads near the ITD that are soft-clipped (an S in the CIGAR), then all reads there:

    samtools view $BAM chr13:28033800-28034200 | awk '$6 ~ /[0-9]+S/' | wc -l
    samtools view -c $BAM chr13:28033800-28034200

    VarDict assembles soft-clipped reads into the longer sequence they came from, so it can call the ITD. Its output goes through a strand-bias test (teststrandbias.R) and is converted to VCF (var2vcf_valid.pl):

    vardict-java -G $REF -N $SAMPLE -b $BAM -f 0.02 -c 1 -S 2 -E 3 -g 4 -th $THREADS $BED \
  | teststrandbias.R \
  | var2vcf_valid.pl -N $SAMPLE -E -f 0.02 > r3.vcf
    bgzip -f r3.vcf && tabix -f -p vcf r3.vcf.gz
    annotate-vcf r3.vcf.gz r3
    benchmark-vcf r3.vcf.gz
    Look inside

    The ITD call (the long ALT allele is cut short here):

    bcftools view -H -r chr13:28033500-28034300 r3.vcf.gz | cut -f1-5 | cut -c1-120
    What to expectThe FLT3 ITD is found. Its observed VAF is usually lower than expected, because reads that only partly overlap the insertion are hard to count.
